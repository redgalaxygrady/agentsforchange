# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

- **Future builders:** young people, late high school and uni students (about 16 to 24), from any discipline. Most cannot code. They use the workspace in short sessions during a four-day, in-person sprint, often on a phone or a borrowed laptop, with a facilitator in the room.
- **Facilitators and community partners** look at the landing page and the downloaded record to understand what a future builder did. They are secondary viewers, not operators.

## Product Purpose

Agents For Change helps young people learn to work with AI by solving real problems for local organisations. A future builder picks an organisation and one of its current challenges, makes something with an AI tool, tries it with real people, improves it, and leaves with a portfolio record of what they decided, what AI did, and what changed.

Success is a future builder who makes a real call about how AI helps, checks what AI tells them, and can show their working to someone else.

## Positioning

The organisation and its people come first, the AI second. Each organisation is introduced by its work, a named contact in their own words, and a short list of current challenges that could use support, not by a single problem statement. The future builder chooses the problem and the person to help, and usually decides what to make after talking to that person. The record shows process, not just a polished result.

## Operating Context

- The landing page is a board of organisations and the problems they'd like help with ("problems to solve", never "jobs"). One organisation, Harbourside Food Rescue, is built out with its own material (a week of donations) and a fact-check kit. The others carry a lighter brief. Anyone can bring their own organisation and challenge.
- After choosing, the future builder moves through Brief, Make & try, and Your record. The AI brief opens in ChatGPT or Claude, or is pasted into any tool.
- Work is saved in the browser. The downloaded record is the backup, so future builders download it at the end of each session.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Vite, React and TypeScript in `studio/`, plain CSS, no backend, no accounts, no AI calls of its own.
- The organisations on the board are made-up examples for practice, and the page must say so. In a real sprint they are swapped for real local organisations.
- Copy is New Zealand English, plain and warm, with no exclamation marks and no "magic" AI language. Say "future builder", "community partner" and "working with AI". Name the practice, not the AI product.

## Brand Commitments

- The name is **Agents For Change**. The brand must not resemble any earlier product it was adapted from.
- Raewyn, the Harbourside coordinator, is shown with a headshot and her own words. There is no video of her.
- Contacts have real-looking headshots where available, with an invented organisation logo shown alongside.

## Evidence on Hand

- Harbourside Food Rescue: Raewyn's headshot (`public/people/harbourside-food-rescue.jpg`, AI-generated), her words, a week of donations, and official food-safety sources.
- Five other made-up organisations with contacts, quotes and challenges in `src/orgs.ts`. They have no headshots yet, so they show initials.
- There are no real testimonials, partners, metrics or results. Do not invent them.

## Product Principles

1. The future builder makes the calls. AI drafts and suggests; the person decides, checks and owns the result.
2. AI output is never the authority. Sources stay visible and the ground rules stop the AI inventing facts.
3. The record tells the truth, and says "not recorded" rather than filling gaps.
4. It works for any discipline. A poster, an audio story or a spreadsheet is as good as a website.
5. Real people and real problems come first.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Works at phone width (390px and 320px) with no horizontal scroll. Keyboard and screen-reader friendly, readable contrast, and plain language for readers who may not be confident with technology.
